Shows and episodes: the recipe. A performed show is made in this order, and each step's tool refuses to be skipped, so treat a refusal as the next piece of work rather than as an obstacle.

1. Format first, even for a one-off. save_show_format is where the decisions that do not change between episodes live: the cast with each character's persona, the pronunciation rules, the timing, the canvas, the loudness targets, the target length with its tolerance, and the look. Then create_episode starts the episode from a brief. Instantiation copies, so editing a format never changes an episode that already exists.

2. Cast for what the script asks. Give every character a persona: who the voice is, as a voice-design instruction - age, character, energy, delivery ("an upbeat man in his thirties, crisp setups, confident punchlines"). Any character who laughs, sighs, gasps, or reacts must be on BreezeBlue/Breeze-TTS-2 with voice_id __engine_default__; a voice that performs no cues will read "laughter" aloud, and write_video_script refuses that cast and names the voice that can. A crowd, an audience, or a room is one character with an ensemble of 3, so its reactions are several voices at once.

3. Write cues, not descriptions of cues. A vocal cue is written in parentheses anywhere in a line - (laughs), (chuckles), (sighs), (gasps), (clears throat), (applause) - and is performed, never read. A line that is only cues, such as an audience's (laughs) (applause), is a reaction turn. A leading (direction) that is not a cue - (dry), (building), (whispering) - steers the delivery and is sent to the voice with its persona. Bracketed notes such as [SFX: door] are never spoken. Write to the target length: spoken English runs about 150 words a minute, so a thirty-second episode is about seventy spoken words plus its reaction beats. Then write_video_script.

4. Narrate. narrate_turns performs each line with its character's voice, persona, and direction, one durable job per line. It skips a line that already has a current take and re-reads one whose words or delivery changed. To hear a long episode quickly, narrate at draft fidelity, listen, then promote only the lines worth keeping with edit_video_timeline's promote_turns_to_final. A draft take can never be exported or released.

5. Listen before judging. listen_to_episode reports the episode as rendered: each line's measured length, who speaks how much, where the silences fall, and the performed length against the target with its tolerance. Never state a listening result that tool did not return, and never estimate a duration or a loudness it reported as unmeasured. An episode outside its length tolerance is a blocking finding: cut or extend lines and re-narrate only those, rather than accepting the length unasked.

6. Give it a picture. A show performed by voices has sound and nothing to look at, and every video deliverable needs a picture. Call video_runtime_status. Where the video generator is present, call generate_episode_clips after narration and write what each shot shows - describe the picture, not the conversation: "a microphone under a warm spotlight, brick wall behind" is a shot, "two friends discussing a lighthouse keeper" is not. Shots are generated in the episode's own aspect and set in the show's world from its look, so describe what is in frame and let the look supply the place; left empty, the look's world yields three views of it. Supply a handful, not one per second: a clip costs about a minute of local compute for under two seconds of footage, and soundAr repeats them across the episode. Where no generator is installed, ensure_episode_cover draws a motion backdrop in the show's palette. The release plan refuses to ship a backdrop while a generator is installed unless the user chooses it, so ask the user before passing accept_backdrop.

7. Score and sound. generate_cue_music composes a planned cue with the local music model and fits it to its length. Use edit_video_timeline's set_lexicon_entry to fix an invented name's pronunciation once instead of respelling it throughout the script.

8. Render, then check. Render the final master. Then call transcribe_and_check_episode with an installed local transcription model: it listens back to every line, measures the master, and reports skipped, inserted, and mispronounced words, a cue read as a word instead of performed, a cue the cast voice could not perform, an off-target master, dead air, and a length outside tolerance. It never silently repairs what it finds. Fix what it reports and check again; the check is recorded against exactly what it listened to, and a release will not accept a check that predates a change.

9. Release only when the plan is ready. plan_episode_release says what the release would contain and names every blocked member with its reason - a missing, stale, or blocking quality check, a backdrop where footage could be made, a draft take, a missing master. Resolve the reasons rather than working around them; accept_findings and accept_backdrop exist for a user who has chosen to, never as a default. Then export_episode_release produces and registers the audio episode with its chapters, the trailer cut from the episode's own strongest narrated moment, and the audiogram, and names any member still blocked. Never present a performed episode as finished while its release plan is blocked, and never call a show done while its audience says the word "laughter".
